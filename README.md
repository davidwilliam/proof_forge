ProofForge turns Meta's Muse models into provers of financial code.

Each task states what financial code must guarantee as locked Lean 4 requirements. ProofForge refutes code that breaks a requirement with a counterexample checked in the Lean kernel, and accepts a fix, written by Muse Spark or by people, only when Lean's Comparator checks its proof with six independent kernels in a fresh sandbox, `leanchecker` replays it, an axiom audit passes, and the executable rebuilt from the same source passes property tests. Every verdict is a Proof Passport, bound by hash to the specification, the source, the executable and the judge image, that anyone can re-run. No proof, no merge.

ProofForge proves the logic of code against a written specification. It does not protect keys, servers, oracles, bridges or other infrastructure, and it does not prove deployed Solidity or Move bytecode.

Built during the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon, organized by Meta and Cerebral Valley: the first commit is from 2026-10-26, the kickoff ([ORIGIN.md](ORIGIN.md), [Built during the hackathon](#built-during-the-hackathon)).

Live site: https://proofforge.dev (with a "Check a fix" panel that sends a real-world candidate to the deployed judge). Machine-readable summaries generated from the committed results: [/llms.txt](https://proofforge.dev/llms.txt) and [/evidence.json](https://proofforge.dev/evidence.json).

## The frontier, measured: Meta's models with ProofForge

What Meta's models reach with ProofForge that they do not reach on their own. Each comparison runs the same Meta model on the same tasks and caps, on its own and with ProofForge, and ProofForge judges both against the same locked specification. [`results/wins/ledger.json`](results/wins/ledger.json), generated by `eval/wins.py` from the raw run files with no model calls, keeps every comparison. 268 comparisons over 569 runs: on 30 the model verified only with ProofForge, on 96 it verified either way and ProofForge made it cheaper or faster, and 53 are gains of other kinds.

- OpenZeppelin `Math.sqrt` behind the Aerodrome first deposit, a task no measured run had verified before ([`results/frontier-2`](results/frontier-2/README.md), preregistered): Muse Spark 1.3 in Muse Code verified 10 of 10 runs with ProofForge, at $1.99 per verified run and a median of 12 minutes, and 0 of 10 on its own after spending $95.45 (one-sided Fisher exact p = 5.4e-6). ProofForgeFin supplies the convergence of Newton's integer square root over any number of steps and the bridge from 256-bit words to natural numbers. On Compound Proposal 62 in the same experiment, every ProofForge run proved 5 of the 8 requirements; on its own, half the final candidates did not build.
- Compound Proposal 62, the COMP reward distribution ([`results/frontier-4`](results/frontier-4/README.md), preregistered): Muse Spark 1.3 in Muse Code verified 8 of 10 runs with ProofForge, at $6.97 per verified run and a median of 18 minutes per verified run, and 0 of 10 on its own after spending $91.74 (one-sided Fisher exact p = 0.00036; 0 of 30 on its own with `frontier-2` and `frontier-3` pooled, p = 0.00000059). Every verified run proves the three bounds over any sequence of operations; no run on its own proved any of them. No measured run of any arm had verified this task before [`results/frontier-3`](results/frontier-3/README.md), where ProofForge verified 2 of 10; `frontier-4` measures the build feedback, skill step and `ProofForgeFin` additions built from those runs (8 of 10 against 2 of 10, p = 0.012). ProofForgeFin supplies `Accumulator.Market`, two-sided reward markets bounded from one case analysis.
- Vyper `sqrt()`, CVE-2025-26622, two preregistered experiments ([`results/hard-task-1`](results/hard-task-1/README.md), [`results/vyper-replication-1`](results/vyper-replication-1/README.md)): Muse Spark 1.3 in Muse Code verified 14 of 15 with ProofForge and 10 of 15 on its own, 2.1x cheaper per verified run ($5.62 against $11.79 at list prices) and 1.7x faster. First experiment 5 of 5 with ProofForge against 2 of 5; replication 9 of 10 against 8 of 10, at lower cost per run with ProofForge (preregistered one-sided Mann-Whitney exact p = 0.022). ProofForgeFin supplies the error bound of Newton's method with floor division, which Lean's core library does not have.
- Balancer V2 ([`results/library-1`](results/library-1/README.md), runs pooled from three experiments): Muse Spark 1.3 with ProofForge and the Muse Wonder 1.0 planner verified 3 of 3, on its own 2 of 5; 6.5x cheaper per verified run ($3.06 against $20.02): Muse Wonder plans the lemmas, Muse Spark proves them.
- Muse Spark Open, Meta's open-weight model, preregistered ([`results/e1-test-open-2`](results/e1-test-open-2/README.md)): on 6 of the 30 frozen test tasks it verified only with ProofForge; 24 of 30 with ProofForge against 22 of 30 on its own, and every final executable with ProofForge passed the property tests (30 of 30, against 22). The first experiment ([`results/e1-test-open-1`](results/e1-test-open-1/e1.md)): 24 of 30 against 21.
- KyberSwap Elastic swap step, the code behind the exploit of 2023-11-22 ([`results/hard-task-2`](results/hard-task-2/README.md), preregistered): with ProofForge every Muse Code final candidate builds, 10 of 10 against 5 of 10 on its own, and the last compiling version proves 3.1 of 8 requirements per run against 1.9 (one-sided Mann-Whitney exact p = 0.014), including the requirement the exploit broke in one run. Neither arm verified a run within the $10 cap.
- Seeded bad candidates ([`results/e2-all-1`](results/e2-all-1/e2.md)): ordinary checking accepted 233 of 247, ProofForge accepted 0 and rejected 0 of 239 good ones.

Every comparison with links to its runs and Passports: [`results/wins/README.md`](results/wins/README.md), and the site's [/frontier](https://proofforge.dev/frontier) page.

## Meta models and tools used

- Muse Code 1.4.14, Meta's coding agent, runs ProofForge as an MCP server ([`integrations/muse/proofforge_mcp.py`](integrations/muse/proofforge_mcp.py)) with the ProofForge skill ([`.agents/skills/proofforge/SKILL.md`](.agents/skills/proofforge/SKILL.md)) and ProofForgeFin in its workspace. `agents/muse_code.py` drives headless `muse exec` runs and `integrations/muse/meter.py` meters every model request. The arm on its own is the same Muse Code with Lean only. The OpenZeppelin, Compound and Vyper results above are Muse Code runs (`results/frontier-2`, `results/frontier-3`, `results/frontier-4`, `results/hard-task-1`, `results/vyper-replication-1`; also `results/muse-code-1`).
- Muse Spark 1.3 (`muse-spark-1.3-contributor`, Meta Model API, Contributor tier) is the model inside Muse Code, and the repairer, implementer and prover of arms A0 (on its own), A1 (the ProofForge harness) and A2 (A1 with the planner).
- Muse Wonder 1.0 (`muse-wonder-1.0`, Meta Model API) is the lemma planner of arm A2; Muse Spark 1.3 proves the lemmas it proposes.
- Muse Spark Open (`meta-models/muse-spark-open-1026`, and `meta-models/muse-spark-open` for twelve rerun tasks), Meta's open-weight model, on Baseten Model APIs: arms A0 and A1 on the 30 frozen test tasks (`results/e1-test-open-1`, `results/e1-test-open-2`).
- Muse Glimmer 30B (`glimmer/muse-glimmer-30b`), self-hosted with vLLM on one Modal H100 ([`infra/modal/glimmer_vllm.py`](infra/modal/glimmer_vllm.py)): arms A0 and A1 on the frozen test tasks (`results/e1-test-glimmer-1`, incomplete).

## Quickstart without Docker

Read the evidence, check committed Passports' hashes, and judge a candidate on the deployed API, with Python 3.12, Node 20 and `jq`:

```sh
git clone https://github.com/davidwilliam/proof_forge && cd proof_forge

# Every committed Passport's passport_sha256 matches its content (no Lean, no Docker)
python3 tests/test_committed_passports.py

# Every figure on the site, recomputed from results/ (no Lean, no Docker)
cd web && pnpm install && pnpm vitest run && cd ..

# The claims, figures, Passport hashes and reproduction commands, as published
jq '.muse_alone_vs_proofforge.rows[] | {incident, muse_alone_A0, with_proofforge}' web/public/evidence.json

# Judge the Cetus same-day hotfix on the deployed judge: REQ-SHLW-EXACT is REFUTED at n = 2^192
jq -n --rawfile s realworld/tasks/rw-cetus-shlw/attacks/pr6_mask_only.lean '{task: "rw-cetus-shlw", source: $s}' \
  | curl -s -X POST -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d @- https://proofforge.fly.dev/api/verify \
  | jq '{status, passport_sha256, requirements: [.requirements[] | {id, status, witness: .witness.message}]}'
```

`GET https://proofforge.fly.dev/api/tasks?split=realworld` lists the real-world tasks with their locked requirements. Re-running a Passport's checks locally (`bin/proofforge verify`) needs Docker and the pinned Lean image; see [Reproduce the Passports](#reproduce-the-passports).

The counterexample stage now produces such refutations automatically from the failing property case: on the Cetus, Euler, Balancer, Knight and Compound ports, 20 of 20 refuted requirements are refuted by a generated theorem accepted by Comparator's six kernels and `leanchecker` (`results/counterexamples-1`, 82 of 85 over 33 candidates including benchmark start candidates). **The Cetus hotfix.** Cetus merged two fixes on the day of the attack; the first, pull request 6 (14:37 UTC), refuses the attack deposit but is REFUTED: `checkedShlw(2^192) = (0, false)`, so a deposit with liquidity × price difference = 2^192 is charged 0 (Passport [`d489c01595c30739`](results/cetus-1/passports/pr6-port.json); the generated refutation `¬ (ShlwExact checkedShlw)`, accepted by all six kernels, is in [`results/counterexamples-1/cetus-pr6-port`](results/counterexamples-1/cetus-pr6-port/passport.json)). Muse's repair, written from the original code, is proved equal to pull request 7 on every 256-bit input (`muse_eq_pr7` in `results/cetus-1/repair-A1-1/Equivalence.lean`, axioms `propext` and `Quot.sound`), and the real pull request 7 Move code returned the same output as the proved repair on all 2,007 inputs. ## ProofForgeFin

`lib/` is a Lean 4 library of proved facts about money code: rounding and fixed point at any scale, share accounting, runs of operations with invariants, conservation over accounts, trading systems, state machines, EVM machine arithmetic, money in minor units, DeFi primitives, sums over lists and positions, integer square roots and Newton iteration, a bridge from machine words to natural numbers, index-based reward accumulators over sequences of actions, and proof automation; 2,051 theorems and 478 definitions in 82 files, with no dependency beyond Lean's core library. All 10,128 constants of the library and its demonstrations as of `results/feedback-library-1` are accepted by the judge's six kernels, and `leanchecker` replays all 87 modules (`harness/fin_kernels.py`); the 71 constants of `ProofForgeFin.Sqrt`, added later, were checked the same way (`results/hard-task-1/kernels-sqrt.json`), and so were `ProofForgeFin.SqrtIter`, `ProofForgeFin.NatSqrt` and `ProofForgeFin.WordBridge` (`results/library-newton-1/kernels.json`), and so were `ProofForgeFin.Accumulator` (`results/library-accumulator-1/kernels-accumulator.json`) and `ProofForgeFin.Accumulator.Market` (`results/library-market-1/kernels-market.json`), and so were `ProofForgeFin.Ledger` and `ProofForgeFin.Accumulator.Market` with the additions of `results/library-compound-gaps-1` (`results/library-compound-gaps-1/kernels.json`). Candidates may import it; the judge vendors the exact version and each Passport records it. `tests/fixtures/library/compound_lib.lean`, a Compound candidate whose three sequence bounds come from the library (written by the build team from the reference solution), is VERIFIED, 8 of 8, including on the deployed judge (Passport `b654d456e1a5eec1057ee259be2c2726fee3a4d3b89d1373dcc3bcbf1c4b7f59`, not committed). Details: [lib/README.md](lib/README.md).

## What is proved and what is not

- Proved: the Lean 4 implementation accepted by the judge meets every locked requirement for every input in the stated domain. For Cetus that is every 256-bit `n` and every `u128` pair of sqrt prices and `u128` liquidity; for Euler, every state reachable from the empty market by any sequence of operations; for Hundred, any history and any further sequence of operations by any actors.
- The requirements are formal Lean statements, not prose. A requirement covers exactly what its Lean statement says.
- Not proved: the Solidity and Move code. Each port is a reviewed hand translation, tested against the real code on the stated inputs (2,007 inputs for Cetus, 501 batches for Balancer, 1,004 sequences for Euler, 501 for Hundred, 503 for Compound); the translation is not machine-checked. Knight Capital's code is not public; its router is reconstructed from the SEC order and nothing in that task is Knight's code.
- Each port models only the logic named in its task README. Cetus: `get_delta_b`, the tick math and the rest of the pool (positions, swaps, fees) are not specified, and whether a deposit with liquidity × price difference = 2^192 was reachable through the pool's entry points under pull request 6 is not claimed. Euler: one asset priced at 1, an eToken exchange rate fixed at 1 and no interest; flash loans, sub-accounts and multiple collateral assets are not modeled. Hundred: only share accounting is modeled; the comptroller, borrowing, liquidation, interest and reserves are not, and the specification does not prevent the first-depositor inflation attack.
- Implement mode turns a precise formal specification into executable code. It does not measure writing code from an informal description. The real-code comparisons were run against the repairs, not against the implementations.
- Loss figures are quoted from the sources each task README cites.
- The JSON wrapper around each proved function is tested, not proved.
- Trust assumptions, listed in every Passport: a VERIFIED requirement relies on the Lean v4.35.0 kernel, compiler and runtime; the protected specification and challenge as reviewed by the owner; Comparator and its sandbox; and the judge image, recorded by digest.

## Breadth

### Benchmark and E1

59 repair tasks in six families (`tasks/`, contract in `docs/TASKS.md`): payouts (9), ledgers (10), transfer batches (10), permissions (10), rate limiters (10), queues (10). 23 are development, 6 validation and 30 test tasks. The test families (ledgers, permissions, queues) never appear in development or in any prompt used to build the agents. Every task has two reference solutions, a buggy start candidate refuted on its binary, and seeded attacks; `harness/validate_task.py` checks all of it.

E1 compares three arms under the same per-task budget (dollars at standard-tier list prices and wall-clock time), scored by one shared evaluator:

- A0: Muse Spark 1.3 compile-and-retry (Lean errors and the axiom audit only)
- A1: Muse Spark 1.3 with the ProofForge harness (behavior-first phase, per-obligation dialogs, snippet checks, binary tests)
- A2: A1 plus Muse Wonder as lemma planner

Development results (`results/e1-dev-2`, 23 tasks, $3 and 45 minutes per run): A0 completed 19, A1 20, A2 21. Every arm completed every rate-limiter and transfer task.

Test results (`results/e1-test-1`, the 30 frozen test tasks, $3 and 60 minutes per run, Muse Spark 1.3 on the Contributor tier):

| Arm | Completed | List $ per completed repair |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | 28 of 30 | 0.87 |
| A1 | 30 of 30 | 0.61 |
| A2 | 30 of 30 | 0.92 |

The test tasks do not separate the arms. A1 and A2 each complete two tasks that A0 does not and none that A0 completes alone (McNemar exact p = 0.50); A1 and A2 complete the same tasks. Both A0 failures are ledger-reversal tasks on which compile-and-retry reached the dollar cap with a final candidate that does not build. Under these budgets Muse Spark completes almost every test task with or without the harness, and the Wonder planner adds cost without a measured completion gain. The 90 runs cost $70.09 at list prices; the amount charged on the Contributor tier was $6.38.

The final test run requires `results/test-freeze.json`. Before making model calls, the runner checks each selected test task and its validation receipt against that approved record and binds the record's hash into the run configuration. Existing validation receipts identify the committed snapshot with which validation was recorded; generating a freeze does not rerun Lean. Resume rejects changes to the experiment configuration.

The approved limits are $3 at standard list prices and 60 minutes per repair run, two concurrent runs, and a $150 batch cap. Unknown usage, lost responses, and HTTP failures retain conservative request reservations. All remaining arms of a task must fit before the task starts; omitted tasks are reported in `coverage.json`. After the repair deadline, finalization may select an already-produced compiling version; final judging is outside the repair budget for every arm. Run from an isolated checkout and keep its inputs fixed:

```sh
python3 eval/e1.py --label test-1 --split test --arms A0,A1,A2 --budget-usd 3 --budget-wall 3600 --parallel 2 --batch-usd 150 --require-clean
```

### E2: what the protected pipeline adds

A repository that keeps proofs next to the code accepts whatever its own build accepts. A repair can prove a weaker statement under a required theorem's name, ship a modified specification, or compile different code from the code that was proved, and the build stays green; Lean checks each of those proofs correctly, but they are proofs of something else.

E2 compares three ways of checking the same artifacts (`results/e2-all-1`; every bad case is a seeded test written by the build team, so this describes this suite, not repairs in general):

| Checking | Seeded bad repairs accepted | Correct repairs rejected |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary checking: the project builds, theorem names are present, the axiom audit is clean | 233 of 247 | 0 of 239 |
| Rebuild and property tests only | 1 of 247 | 0 of 239 |
| ProofForge | 0 of 247 | 0 of 239 |

The rebuild and its tests already reject almost every seeded attack: 229 change behavior on inputs the generator produces, and 17 do not build against the locked specification. The one that passes is `formal/attacks/g2_bulk_rounding.lean`: payouts to seven or more recipients drop the remainder, the test generator never produces more than five recipients, and the attack proves `req_fair_shares`, `req_effects` and `req_conservation` restated for fewer than seven recipients. Comparator rejects the restated statement; the counterexample ($1.00 to seven recipients pays 14 cents each and loses 2 cents) is in `formal/attacks/g2_bulk_rounding.counterexample.json`. A proof covers every input; tests cover the inputs someone thought to generate.

The 239 good cases are 118 reference-solution checks, 118 generated refactors of them (helpers renamed, comments added) and 3 accepted model repairs; tasks that share a base specification reuse files, so they come from 67 distinct sources. No specification-weakening attempt was observed in 138 development repair runs; every bad case here is seeded.

### Natural failures in Muse-written candidates

`results/natural-1` classifies every intermediate candidate the Muse agents saved in E1 and the payout development runs: 5,860 versions from 234 runs on 53 tasks, with no model calls and no edits by the build team. The 293 distinct implementations were each run on 62,001 generated cases (the standard suite, 20 further seeds and a stretched generator); none passes the standard tests while violating a requirement on a confirmed witness, and the same search finds 338 violating cases in the seeded bulk-rounding attack used as a control. No candidate changed a required theorem statement, redefined a protected definition or declared an axiom. 51 candidates in 2 runs used `native_decide` and 30 in 10 runs lacked a required theorem; the judge rejected a representative of each of the 15 flagged groups. The witnesses are defined by the task oracle, the inputs are generated only, and the corpus covers one model under this harness, which never asked the agents to evade the checks.

## How it works

A component's requirements are written once as Lean 4 predicates over its implementation and locked by hash. A Muse model fixes the buggy code, or writes it from a stub, and proves every requirement. The candidate counts only if the official checker accepts it in a fresh sandbox and the executable rebuilt from the same source passes property tests derived from the same requirements. ProofForge judges every candidate against the locked challenge with Lean's `lake comparator` in a bubblewrap sandbox (six kernels in paranoid mode: Lean default and paranoid, lean4lean, nanoda, con-leche, con-ron), replays it with `leanchecker`, audits its axioms, rejects code-override constructs, and tests the executable rebuilt from the locked project. Each requirement gets a status: VERIFIED, REFUTED with a concrete witness, or UNRESOLVED. For a REFUTED requirement, the counterexample stage turns the witness into a theorem, the negation of the locked requirement about the candidate's definitions on that input, checks it with the same six kernels, `leanchecker` and the axiom audit, and records it in the Passport ([docs/COUNTEREXAMPLES.md](docs/COUNTEREXAMPLES.md)). The result is a Proof Passport: per-requirement statuses, hashes, commands, axioms, trust assumptions and a one-command reproduction. Every step, with its exact command, is described in [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).

### Meta models and their roles

Muse Spark 1.3 and Muse Wonder 1.0 are called through the Meta Model API, Muse Spark Open through Baseten Model APIs and Muse Glimmer 30B through a self-hosted vLLM server on Modal (`agents/meta_client.py`). Muse Code runs Muse Spark 1.3 with ProofForge as its MCP server ([Meta models and tools used](#meta-models-and-tools-used)).

- Muse Spark 1.3 (`muse-spark-1.3-contributor`) is the repairer, implementer and prover. It fixes behavior against the binary tests first, then proves each requirement through tools: compile a snippet, bank proved lemmas, check per-theorem status, run tests on the compiled binary.
- Muse Wonder 1.0 (`muse-wonder-1.0`) is the lemma planner in arm A2: when an obligation resists direct proof, it proposes helper lemma statements that Spark then proves. On the E1 test tasks it did not change completion and added cost. Of the real-world repairs, Balancer and Knight were accepted with the planner (A2), and the Knight implementation used it; Cetus, Euler and Hundred used none.
- Costs are tracked at two prices (`agents/budget.py`): Muse Spark 1.3 at standard list prices ($1.25 input, $0.15 cached input, $4.25 output per million tokens), which the per-run dollar caps use, and as charged on the Contributor tier ($0.10, $0.002 and $0.20).

### Deployment

The API and judge run on a Fly.io Machine (performance-4x, 8 GB, region iad), the front end on Vercel. Fly Machines are microVMs with their own Linux kernel, so Comparator's bubblewrap sandbox runs unchanged as an unprivileged user. Both deploy scripts build from the committed tree only (`infra/fly/deploy.sh`, `infra/vercel/deploy.sh`); Passports record the judge image by registry digest.

Live jobs run Muse Spark 1.3 on its Contributor tier, on synthetic tasks only, with Muse Wonder as planner. Controls (`server/controls.py`): per-client daily quotas and a daily live spending cap at the prices actually charged, stored on the data volume, a per-job dollar cap at standard list prices and a wall-clock cap, concurrency limits that answer 503 with Retry-After, request-wide deadlines on checks (a check stopped by its deadline is UNRESOLVED, never REFUTED), and an operator switch to replay-only mode that can also stop running live agents.

Measured on the deployed host:

- [Host smoke test](reports/host-smoke/fly-iad.json): the recorded repair is VERIFIED by all six kernels in the sandbox; the seeded bulk-rounding candidate passes its tests and is UNRESOLVED; a complete-proof attack and an `implemented_by` override are REFUTED.
- Live repairs on the host, Muse Spark 1.3 with the Wonder planner, all eight requirements VERIFIED: [standard tier](reports/host-smoke/fly-live-repair.json), 1,620 s, $2.74 charged; [Contributor tier](reports/host-smoke/fly-live-repair-contributor.json), 1,756 s, $0.44 charged ($0.08 Spark, $0.36 Wonder; $1.84 at standard list prices).
- [Reproduction from the site on another architecture](reports/reproduction/fly-passport-on-arm64.txt): every Passport page offers the Passport and the candidate source it checked. In a fresh clone on an Apple Silicon Mac, `bin/proofforge verify passport.json --candidate Solution.lean` reproduced every hash and status of a Passport issued by the host, except the executable hash: the host builds for linux/amd64, an executable built for another CPU architecture has a different hash, and `verify` compares that hash only on the same architecture after rebuilding and re-testing the executable.
- Demo reliability (`infra/reliability.py`): each session loads the four web pages, replays the accepted recording to a fresh check, runs the hidden challenge, calls both payout builds and fetches the Passport, through the API. [Run 1](reports/reliability/fly-vercel-1.json): 19 of 20 sessions; session 10 returned HTTP 500 from a race on the job record, fixed by atomic replacement with a regression test. [Run 2](reports/reliability/fly-vercel-2.json), after the fix: 20 of 20 sessions, about 30 s each. Both runs passed three failure drills: replay-only mode refuses live jobs while replays continue; five simultaneous verifications give two results and three 503 refusals with nothing left running; a restart during a job ends it with a stated reason and the next session succeeds.
- [Failure drills](reports/drills/fly-1.json) (`infra/drills.py`): a Lean syntax error, a required proof left as `sorry`, an empty file and an `implemented_by` override are UNRESOLVED; the buggy candidate with a comment telling the checker to report VERIFIED is REFUTED, and a correct repair with the same comment is VERIFIED; malformed or out-of-bounds requests get 4xx answers. On the local judge ([report](reports/drills/local-1.json), `infra/drills_local.py`), a Passport edited after issue, a stale Passport from an earlier lock and a source file changed after the check (by one comment, or back to the buggy code) are not reproduced, and a missing or widened Comparator configuration, in the checker checkout or shipped with the candidate, is a stated rejection. Model API failures (rate limiting, server errors, malformed bodies, a rejected key, malformed tool arguments) are drilled against a local stand-in in `tests/test_model_failures.py`: retried and charged at their worst case, a persistent outage ends at the budget cap.
- [Browser walk-through](reports/browser-walk/vercel-1.json) (`infra/browser-walk.js`, Chromium): the replay reaches a VERIFIED Passport in 33 s at 80x, the hidden challenge and both payout builds answer, and all five pages render at 1440 px and 390 px in light and dark with no console errors, failed requests or horizontal scrolling. Its first pass found the results and Passport pages scrolling sideways on a phone; fixed before this run.
